A high-income strategy that ignores risk is just leverage in disguise. Two
disciplines protect the income stream: hedging the portfolio, and structuring
the options program for tax efficiency.

## Hedging the strategy

Covered calls cushion drawdowns by the premium collected, but they do not
eliminate downside in the underlying. We **hedge the investment strategy**
explicitly — managing tail risk and the correlated drawdowns that come with a
frontier-tech, high-beta book — so that a single volatility shock does not
impair the ability to keep distributing income.

## The Section 1256 tax advantage

Certain options and index products receive favorable treatment under
**Section 1256** of the U.S. tax code: qualifying contracts are marked to market
and taxed at a blended **60% long-term / 40% short-term** rate, regardless of
holding period — a materially better outcome than fully short-term gains for an
actively traded book.

We **study the NEOS model for the Section 1256 advantage** and aim to structure
the strategy so that as much of the income as possible qualifies for this
treatment, maximizing *after-tax* yield rather than headline yield.
