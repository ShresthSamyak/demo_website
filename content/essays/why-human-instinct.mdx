The name Human Instinct reflects our central belief about markets.

Stock prices do not move solely because of financial statements.

They move because of expectations.

They move because of narratives.

They move because of optimism, pessimism, excitement, fear, and conviction.

History is filled with examples of companies whose stock prices surged long
before their revenues justified investor enthusiasm. It is equally filled with
examples of companies whose fundamentals remained strong while their stock prices
collapsed due to changing sentiment.

Markets are voting machines before they become weighing machines.

Understanding how people think is often as important as understanding what a
company earns.

Human Instinct seeks to identify moments when psychology and reality diverge.

We seek opportunities where human behavior creates mispricing.

We seek durable cash flow from productive assets.

We seek exposure to transformational technological change.

Most importantly, we believe that in a world increasingly dominated by
algorithms, the ultimate edge remains understanding the people who use them.

That edge is Human Instinct.
